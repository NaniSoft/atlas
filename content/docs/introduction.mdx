Atlas is a digital twin platform. It builds **living representations of real systems** — systems, assets, and operations — so you can ask questions of a model instead of assembling an answer by hand. Its first proven domain is the **IT estate**: directories, HR systems, databases, and applications turned into one queryable graph.

That is the whole frame, and the order matters. *Digital twin platform* is what Atlas is for. *The IT estate* is what it demonstrably does today. Nothing in these docs claims a second domain before it exists.

## The twin

A twin is a graph, and the graph is **produced, not assembled**. Every fact lands raw, is conformed, and is promoted layer by layer — raw in Bronze, clean in Silver, published as Gold — until it becomes part of the twin. Directories, HR systems, databases, and applications become nodes. Memberships, grants, and activity become edges. The estate finally agrees with itself.

Because the twin comes off a real data platform — ingestion, transformation, quality gates, versioned layers — it stays trustworthy as the estate changes. You can reproduce last quarter's twin exactly.

## What it is not

Atlas is not a dashboard and not a CMDB export. The twin answers questions **by traversal** — walk the edges — rather than by stitching reports together. And it is not a finished product line: one use case is available today, and the rest are labelled **planned** wherever they appear on this site.

## Status

- **Access traversal** — the flagship use case, available today. See [Access traversal, start to finish](/docs/guides/access-traversal).
- **Blast radius** and **stale and unused access** — planned, designed on the same graph. No new connectors.

## See it run

The [playground](https://playground.nanisoft.com) is a fully mocked, in-browser tour of the twin: it replays the flagship audit end to end against a seeded estate — twenty-two steps, from schema authoring to the highlighted traversal. Nothing to install, and no real data anywhere in it.

Atlas's architecture is also a real artifact: the component model that names every codename, its phase, and the off-the-shelf product it wraps is a published package, and the docs here are written from it rather than from a diagram.
