Two cross-cutting platform concerns, both composed rather than built: who may ask, and what the components may read.

## OPA — authorization decisions

**OPA** — the [Open Policy Agent](https://www.openpolicyagent.org/) — returns authorization decisions. It is stateless: it reads a Rego policy and returns allow or deny.

[Atlas](/docs/architecture/in-house-components) consults OPA with the requester and the use case before traversing. In the platform OPA is surfaced as a zone inside the Atlas overlay rather than as its own tool — it is a decision the engine consumes, not a screen an analyst visits.

Policy sits **in front of** the graph; see [Traversal and policy](/docs/concepts/traversal-and-policy) for why that ordering matters.

## OpenBao — secrets

**OpenBao** is [Vault-API-compatible](https://openbao.org/) secrets management. It provides secrets to the components that need them — the engine and the connector platform among them — so credentials never live in a component's own configuration or, worse, in the lakehouse.

## Why neither is built in-house

Authorization and secret storage are exactly the kind of problem where a hand-rolled answer is a liability rather than a differentiator: both are security-critical, both are well-trodden, and both are auditable by third parties precisely because they are standard. The estate's *specifics* — which use cases exist, who may run them, which fields are sensitive — live in policy and schema that are ours, expressed in formats the standard tools consume.

See [the integration ledger](/docs/reference/integration-ledger) for both entries' roles.
