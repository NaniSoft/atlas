Atlas is **composed, not written**. Sixteen proven open-source products carry the platform; four components are built in-house. The whole thing is named in a published component model — the same model that drives the [playground](https://playground.nanisoft.com) and the [integration ledger](/docs/reference/integration-ledger).

## Codenames, with the real product beneath

Most of the platform runs under a codename: **Trailhead** is Airflow, **Forge** is Spark + dbt, **Bedrock** is Nessie over Iceberg, **Overlook** is Trino, **Blueprint** is DataGerry.

The codename is a seam, not a disguise. Every codenamed entry carries the real product with it — in the ledger, in the architecture docs, and in the playground's own component inspector. The point is portability: the platform's contracts are the codenames, so the product underneath can be swapped without rewriting the estate's model of itself.

Two things the codenames are **not**:

- **Not a fork.** Every off-the-shelf product runs unmodified — integrated through its APIs, configured, never patched.
- **Not a rebrand of our own work.** The four things Nanisoft actually builds are named plainly, with no codename at all.

## The four built in-house

| Component | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Atlas** | The core engine: traversal API, authorization enforcement, audit log, use-case step serving. Backed by a Postgres operational store. |
| **Compass** | The traversal UI: explore the twin as a graph, with drill-into-node detail. |
| **DataGerry Bridge** | Glue that syncs authored schema into the lakehouse and refreshes the engine's schema cache. |
| **Scout** | Connectors for the few internal source systems no off-the-shelf catalog covers. |

Everything else — orchestration, transform, storage and catalog, query, dashboards, infrastructure, GitOps, secrets, observability, and the connector platform — is composed. See [In-house components](/docs/architecture/in-house-components) and the [integration ledger](/docs/reference/integration-ledger).

## Why compose

Because the hard problems here are not novel. Orchestrating DAGs, conforming identities, cataloguing a lakehouse, and querying it are solved problems with mature, battle-tested answers. Building them again would spend the effort that belongs to the graph.

Composition has a price and it is paid honestly: the platform carries the integration surface of sixteen products, and it depends on their release cycles. [Anchor](/docs/architecture/delivery) declares the infrastructure as code and [Conveyor](/docs/architecture/delivery) delivers it, so that surface is reviewable and reproducible rather than tribal.
